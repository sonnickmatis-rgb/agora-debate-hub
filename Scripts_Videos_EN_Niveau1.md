## Level 1 · Module 1 · Recognising AI
**File:** `niveau1-module1-en.mp4`
**On-screen text:** "Where is AI?" · "One criterion: inference" · "Already in force"

Hello, and welcome to this training on the AI Act, the European regulation on artificial intelligence.

Let's start with a simple question: where is AI in your work? You're probably thinking of ChatGPT or Copilot. But AI also hides elsewhere: in project management software that starts summarising your meetings, in a tool that sorts documents, in a feature that appeared after a simple update. Very often, we use it without even realising it.

Yet the AI Act has been in force since the first of August, twenty twenty-four. It is a directly applicable European regulation: it applies to your company without waiting for a national law. And it doesn't matter whether the tool comes from the United States or anywhere else: as soon as it is used in the European Union, the regulation applies.

So, how do you recognise an AI system? Remember one word: inference. An AI system works out a result by itself from the data it receives: a summary, a prediction, a recommendation. Conversely, a tool that only applies rules written by humans is not an AI system.

In this module, you will follow Léa, a project manager who has just joined Nexalis, a digital services company based in Belval. With her, you will learn to spot AI, to know who is concerned, and to recognise the few cases that fall outside the regulation. Ready? Let's go!

## Level 1 · Module 2 · Training: Article 4
**File:** `niveau1-module2-en.mp4`
**On-screen text:** "AI literacy · Article 4" · "A best-efforts obligation" · "The Omnibus didn't postpone everything"

In this second module, let's talk about you, and about training.

Article four of the AI Act deals with what is called AI literacy. The idea is simple: companies that use AI must take measures so that the people who use it understand what they are doing. This applies to employees, but also to interns, temporary workers and contractors working for the company.

This obligation has existed since February twenty twenty-five. In July twenty twenty-six, a new European text, the AI Omnibus, reworded it: it is now a best-efforts obligation. In other words, the company must take action to train you, without having to guarantee a specific level for each person. But be careful: training has not become optional.

And there's no need for an official certificate. An internal register, showing who was trained, when, and with what result, is enough. The training you are taking right now is part of it.

You may also hear that the Omnibus postponed everything. That's not true. Only the rules on high-risk uses have been pushed back, to December twenty twenty-seven and August twenty twenty-eight. The prohibitions, AI literacy and transparency already apply today.

In this module, you will separate fact from fiction, and leave with a clear timeline of the key dates.
